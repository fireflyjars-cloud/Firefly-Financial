✓ Applied to categories below

There's no single official rule for splitting an event budget — every planner's numbers vary a bit. But researching wedding-industry data, corporate/upscale event guidance, and general party-planning sources together shows a consistent pattern: smaller, casual gatherings put most of the budget into food, while larger, more formal events shift more of the budget toward the venue and photography — a backyard party doesn't need a rented hall, but a 100-person celebration usually does, and that pulls dollars away from catering even as the DJ and decorations become relatively cheaper per guest. Below is a synthesized starting-point split across three guest-count tiers, built from that research:

Total100%100%100%

Why it shifts: a $600 flat DJ fee spread across 20 guests is $30/head — spread across 40 guests, it's $15/head. That's the fixed-cost effect, and it's why DJ/sound and decorations shrink as a share of the budget when guest count grows. Venue and photography move the other way — a small home-based gathering barely needs a venue line at all, but a 100-person celebration usually means a rented hall and fuller photo coverage, which eats into catering's share even though the food per guest hasn't changed. This is a starting point, not a rule — a backyard gathering with a free venue or a hotel-based event with a room block will shift these numbers further.     💡 Firefly Planning Tips

    🏨 Ask About the Room Block → Event Space Discount

    If you're planning your event at a hotel where your guests are also staying overnight, ask about a room block — a set number of rooms reserved for your group, usually at a discounted nightly rate. Many hotels will also waive or discount the rental fee for your event space once you book enough room nights (sometimes called a rooms-to-space ratio). This is a real, common practice — but it's negotiated, not automatic, and it's rarely 100% free.

    • Ask directly: "If I block X rooms, what event space comes with it?"
    • Get the room-to-space ratio and any food & beverage minimum in writing.
    • Watch for an attrition clause — if your group doesn't fill the block, you may be charged a penalty for the empty rooms.

    Firefly tip: Budget the room block as a "negotiate this" line, not a guaranteed savings line, until the contract is signed.

    🗓️ Off-Peak Dates Save Real Money

    Venues and vendors often charge less for dates outside their busiest season, or for a Friday/Sunday instead of a Saturday. Before you lock in a date, ask what the rate difference is — it can be significant enough to move an entire category of your budget.

    🛟 Build In a Buffer

    Set aside 10–15% of your total budget as a contingency line before you allocate the rest. Almost every event has at least one surprise cost — better to plan for it than to raid another category when it shows up.

    ✦ A Word Before You Celebrate

    A good event doesn't need to cost more than you can see coming. Name the categories. Know the numbers. Whether it's just you covering it or a whole circle of people showing up to help — plan the glow, don't just hope for it.
